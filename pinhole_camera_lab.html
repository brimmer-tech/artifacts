How it works

Light bounces off the tree in every direction. Only a thin beam from each spot on the tree fits through the pinhole, so each spot lands in its own place on the back wall.

Those beams cross at the hole, so the picture arrives upside down.

Try this: click the pinhole, then the shutter. Or close the shutter and watch the light stop.

Camera means room“Camera obscura” is Latin for “dark room.” The first cameras were whole rooms with a hole in the wall.
Slow on purposeA pinhole lets in very little light, so pictures take seconds to many minutes instead of a split second.
Always in focusWith no lens, near and far things are equally (softly) sharp. There’s nothing to focus.

      Try it without building anything

      Leaf pinholes: on a sunny day, look at the round spots of light under a leafy tree. Each spot is a tiny picture of the sun made by a gap between leaves.

      Room camera: cover a bedroom window completely with cardboard and foil, poke a hole about 1 cm wide, and wait a few minutes for your eyes to adjust. The outside world appears upside down on the opposite wall.

      Eclipse viewer: poke a pinhole in a card and let sunlight fall through it onto a second card. Never look at the sun itself, through the hole or otherwise.
