How the tank works

Six systems that make this tank behave like a real one. Tap any of them to learn more.

1The nitrogen cycle

Fish waste and leftover food make ammonia, which is toxic. Bacteria living in the filter turn ammonia into nitrite, which is also toxic, and then turn nitrite into nitrate, which is mostly safe.

A brand-new tank has almost no bacteria, so ammonia rises first, then nitrite, until the colonies grow. Fishkeepers call this “new tank syndrome.” Even in an established tank, nitrate slowly builds up until you change some of the water.

Try it: Open Water test and equipment in the tank panel. When ammonia or nitrite turns orange, change 30% of the water and feed lightly until the bacteria catch up.

2Stocking limits

Every fish adds waste, and bigger fish add more. The filter bacteria can only process so much, so in a crowded tank, ammonia builds up faster than they can remove it. Crowded fish also get stressed, which makes them more likely to get sick.

Try it: Keep Stocking under 100%. If the tank is overcrowded, click a fish and choose Remove from tank.

3Temperature

Tropical fish need warm water, about 74 to 82°F. Colder water makes them sluggish and more likely to catch diseases. Water that’s too warm, above 85°F, holds less oxygen, so fish gasp at the surface.

Tap water is cooler than the tank, so the temperature dips after a water change until the heater warms it back up.

Try it: Use the heater’s − and + buttons in Water test and equipment.

4Growth and lifespan

New fish arrive young and grow into adults over about three hours, as long as they’re healthy and well fed. Size matters in a tank: fully grown angelfish will eat small fish like neon tetras, especially when they’re hungry.

Try it: Click a fish to see its Stage. Keep your angelfish fed to protect your tetras.

5Disease

Stress from cold water, dirty water, crowding, or hunger weakens fish. Stressed fish can catch ich, a common parasite that looks like grains of salt on the body. It spreads to nearby fish, and infected fish scratch themselves against the sand.

New fish can bring ich with them, which is why real fishkeepers keep new arrivals in a separate quarantine tank first.

Try it: Raise the heater to 84°F to clear ich naturally, or use Treat for ich. Medicine works faster but sets back the filter bacteria a little.

6Plants

Live plants absorb nitrate and a little ammonia, and they compete with algae for those same nutrients. Plants need light to do this. Algae grow fastest when nitrate is high and the lights stay on for a long time.

Try it: Use Add a plant in Water test and equipment, and switch the lights off when nobody is watching.
