Robotics Lab: Sense, Think, Act

Every robot, from a robot vacuum to a Mars rover, runs the same loop over and over. It senses the world, thinks about what it sensed, then acts. Work through the four stations to build that loop one piece at a time.

Motors and differential drive

This robot has two wheels, each with its own motor, and no steering wheel. It turns by running its wheels at different speeds. That’s called differential drive, and it’s how robot vacuums, most classroom robots, and tanks get around.

Turning the world into numbers

A robot can’t see the room the way you do. Each of its three distance sensors (like the ultrasonic or infrared sensors on real robots) reports a single number: how far away the nearest thing is in that direction. The bump sensor reports only yes or no.

Check yourself Why would a robot vacuum with only a bump sensor be a worse robot than one with distance sensors?

A bump sensor only learns about an obstacle after hitting it. Distance sensors let the robot notice something in advance and react before contact, which is safer and faster.

        Why does “spin left until clear” sometimes trap the robot in a corner?
        
Simple rules react only to what the sensors read right now, so the robot can swing from one wall straight into another. Better programs compare options (like “toward the more open side”) or remember what they just did.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Feedback: following a line

      
A row of five light sensors looks at the floor just ahead of the robot. From them the robot works out the error: how far the line is from its center. Then it steers to shrink that error. Check yourself
        Why does a faster robot often need a different Kp?
        
At higher speed the robot travels farther before each correction takes effect, so the error grows faster in curves. It needs stronger steering, but too strong and it overshoots the line and wobbles back and forth.

      

    

  


  

    
Vocabulary

    

      
Sensor
A part that measures something about the world and turns it into a number.

      
Actuator
A part that makes the robot do something physical, like a motor.

      
Controller
The program that turns sensor readings into motor commands.

      
Differential drive
Steering by running two wheels at different speeds.

      
Threshold
A cutoff value that decides when a rule kicks in.

      
Feedback control
Constantly measuring the error and correcting to make it smaller.
